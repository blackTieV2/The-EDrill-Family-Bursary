# The EDrill Family Bursary - Legal and Compliance Action Note

Working status: pre-launch action note  
Purpose: identify the legal, tax, payroll, privacy, governance, and communication gates that must be closed before The EDrill Family Bursary is launched.  
Important: this is not legal advice. It is an internal control note to help EDrill ask the right questions and avoid launching the scheme on weak assumptions.

## 1. Executive Position

The safest immediate framing is:

> The EDrill Family Bursary is an internal, company-funded, discretionary education support scheme for eligible families connected with EDrill Thailand operations.

The first launch should not describe the scheme as a charity, foundation, public fund, registered society, or donation-funded programme.

Why this matters:

- The current intent is company-funded support, not public or member fundraising.
- A constitution-style legal framework may imply a separate body before EDrill has decided to create one.
- A future Singapore Registry of Societies or fundraising model can be preserved without prematurely adopting that structure.
- Thai payroll/tax/privacy treatment must be confirmed before payments begin.

Recommended first governing document name:

- Use "Bursary Charter" for the company-funded launch.
- Reserve "Constitution" for a future separate society, foundation, association, or member-funded structure.

## 2. Current Regulatory Anchors

### Singapore - Registry of Societies

Official ROS guidance states that a society is a club, company, partnership, or association of 10 or more persons, whatever its nature or object, and not registered under another law. A society of 10 or more persons should seek registration unless an exemption applies.

Practical implication:

- The Phase 1 EDrill company-funded bursary does not appear to need ROS registration merely because the company internally funds a bursary.
- ROS becomes relevant if the scheme becomes a member association, employee/member club, or separate body of 10 or more persons not already registered under another law.

Official ROS registration materials also require, among other things, a society name, registered address, constitution in English, and key office bearers such as President, Secretary, and Treasurer for online verification.

Practical implication:

- If EDrill later creates a Singapore society, the future constitution should be drafted using the ROS template as a starting point.
- The future model will need office bearers, members, a registered address, constitution, accounting rules, and annual reporting.

### Singapore - Annual Reporting If A Society Is Later Formed

Official ROS guidance requires annual return submission and, depending on income or expenditure thresholds, audited accounts.

Practical implication:

- The Phase 1 scheme should keep clean records even though it is not yet a society.
- A future society model will need stronger accounting discipline than an ordinary HR benefit.

### Singapore - Fundraising

Official Charity Portal guidance defines fundraising appeals broadly where money or property is requested or received for charitable, benevolent, or philanthropic purposes. Singapore fundraising appeals, whether online or offline and whether for local or foreign purposes, may be regulated.

For local charitable purposes, public fundraising disclosures may need to be submitted at least seven working days before the appeal starts where applicable.

For foreign charitable purposes, official guidance states that fundraising activities for foreign charitable causes require a permit from the Commissioner of Charities before the fundraising appeal starts. Official guidance also identifies record-keeping, accounting, audit, and proceeds-application requirements for foreign charitable fundraising.

Practical implication:

- Do not solicit donations in Singapore at Phase 1.
- Do not add "donate", "contribute", "fundraiser", or similar wording to LinkedIn or internal communications unless the fundraising structure has been reviewed.
- If the future plan is to raise money in Singapore for children in Thailand or Myanmar, that may be treated as fundraising for a foreign charitable or benevolent purpose and must be separately checked.

### Thailand - Tax, Payroll, Labour, and Privacy

Public English sources from the Thai Revenue Department confirm that assessable income includes money, property, or other benefits that can be computed into monetary value, and that employment income includes money, property, or benefits derived from employment. Public source review also identified an education-award exemption, but did not conclusively settle whether an employer-funded bursary for an employee's child qualifies, especially where the award is connected to employment and paid to the employee's bank account.

Practical implication:

- Bursary payments should be treated as tax liable to the recipient.
- Recipients are responsible for their own tax position.
- EDrill should not describe the bursary as tax-free.
- Finance/Payroll confirmation of company-side processing, withholding, reporting, payslip, and accounting treatment remains a launch gate.
- Direct employee bank-account payment should be treated as a controlled recipient-taxable payment route requiring company-side Finance/Payroll processing confirmation.
- Payment through a labour-hire agency should be separately confirmed for accounting, withholding/reporting, onward-payment evidence, and labour-law treatment.

Thailand labour-hire note:

The English translation of Thailand's Labour Protection Act includes Section 11/1, under which an entrepreneur using recruited/contract workers in its business operation may be deemed an employer for relevant labour-protection purposes and must provide fair benefits and welfare without discrimination to contract employees performing work in the same manner as employees.

Practical implication:

- The fact that labour-hire crew hold contracts with an agency does not fully remove EDrill from labour-law risk.
- The Bursary should use objective eligibility criteria, agency-neutral wording, and Legal-approved controls.
- Payment via the labour-hire agency is helpful for relationship management, but it does not remove the need for Thai labour advice.

The scheme will also involve children's identity data, school records, family relationship data, salary/financial data, and possibly hardship information.

Practical implication:

- Thai privacy requirements must be checked before forms are issued.
- The application form should collect only necessary data.
- Applicant consent should cover collection, verification, review, storage, and limited internal transfer of data.
- Publication of beneficiary names or photos should require separate written consent.

## 4. Thailand Questions To Send To Finance / Payroll / Legal

These should be answered in writing before the first award.

1. Given the working position that payments are tax liable to the recipient, what payroll/reporting category should be used?
2. Does the answer change if EDrill pays the school directly?
3. Does the answer change if EDrill reimburses the employee against receipts?
4. Does the answer change if the beneficiary is a labour-hire agency employee's child?
5. Should payment be processed via payroll or accounts payable?
6. What withholding, payslip, reporting, or year-end tax treatment is required, if any?
7. What evidence is required for audit and deductibility?
8. Are school fee invoices/receipts sufficient evidence?
9. Can payments cover books, uniforms, devices, transport, meals, or tutoring?
10. Does the scheme need to be included in any employee welfare policy or work rules?
11. What wording is needed to preserve the scheme as discretionary and non-contractual?
12. What Thai privacy notices and consent wording are required?
13. Can EDrill verify enrolment or fee records directly with schools?
14. Are there restrictions on transferring applicant data to Singapore management?
15. What retention period should apply to application documents?

## 5. Labour-Hire Agency Risk Note

Including eligible employees of the appointed labour-hire agency at the time of application who are assigned to EDrill rigs may be morally and operationally attractive, but it is the part of the scheme most likely to create legal ambiguity.

Key risks:

- Wording may imply labour-hire agency employees are EDrill employees.
- Direct payment to labour-hire staff may create employment or contractor-management concerns.
- Labour-hire agencies may need to verify employment, service, assignment records, and onward payment.
- The benefit may create expectations among other contractor groups.
- Public communication may blur the contractor relationship.

Recommended controls:

- Use the wording "eligible employees of the appointed labour-hire agency at the time of application who are assigned to EDrill rigs".
- Avoid "our employees" for labour-hire agency personnel.
- Obtain written acknowledgement from the relevant labour-hire agency before including its employees.
- Have the agency verify employment status and EDrill Operations verify rig assignment/continuity.
- If payment is routed through the agency, require onward-payment confirmation if Finance requires it.
- Keep the labour-hire award category separate in the procedure, award letter, and reporting.

## 6. Recommended Payment-Control Position

Working route based on current user instruction:

1. EDrill employee awards paid quarterly to the employee's nominated bank account.
2. Labour-hire crew awards paid quarterly to the relevant labour-hire agency for onward payment to its employee.
3. Direct school payment or reimbursement retained as possible fallback/exception routes if Finance/Payroll/Legal require them.

Controls needed because of this route:

- Retain school enrolment and education-cost evidence even where payment is made to an employee bank account.
- State that payment is subject to applicable tax, payroll, withholding, and reporting treatment.
- Require quarterly eligibility/evidence checks before each tranche.
- Require labour-hire agency acknowledgement and onward-payment confirmation where applicable.
- Do not describe the award as tax-free.

Counterpoint:

- Direct employee bank payment is administratively easier and supports families' cash flow.
- It may, however, increase the risk that the payment is treated as an employment-related cash benefit.
- Payment through an agency may preserve relationship boundaries, but only if the agency's onward-payment and reporting obligations are clear.

Practical compromise:

- Keep the user-confirmed direct/agency payment route in the draft.
- Build in evidence, quarterly review, tax/payroll, and agency controls.
- Retain school direct payment/reimbursement as fallback routes if Finance/Payroll later determines that the approved payment route creates unacceptable processing risk.

## 7. Public Communication Controls

Do not publish a LinkedIn post until:

- the scheme charter is approved;
- the payment route is confirmed;
- tax/payroll processing is confirmed;
- privacy wording is approved;
- labour-hire agency wording is approved, if contractor personnel are mentioned;
- the first award cycle process is ready.

Avoid these words at Phase 1:

- charity
- foundation
- registered
- public fund
- donation
- fundraising
- guaranteed
- entitlement

Safer language:

- company-funded
- discretionary
- education support
- eligible families
- Thailand operations
- administered by Bangkok HR
- subject to annual review and available bursary places

## 8. Future Singapore ROS / Fundraising Pathway

If the scheme later "catches on" and EDrill wants to raise funds from employees, members, or a wider community in Singapore, the future workstream should answer:

1. Is the future body a society, company limited by guarantee, charity, trust, or internal employee welfare fund?
2. Will it have 10 or more members and therefore need ROS assessment?
3. Will it collect money for charitable, benevolent, or philanthropic purposes?
4. Will the beneficiaries be in Singapore, Thailand, Myanmar, or multiple countries?
5. Will contributions be private/internal only or public?
6. Will public fundraising disclosure, permit, audit, or record-keeping rules apply?
7. Who owns the EDrill name and brand if the body becomes separate?
8. What happens to funds on dissolution?

Recommended future-readiness clause for Phase 1 documents:

> The Company may review the structure, funding model, administration, or legal form of the Bursary from time to time, including whether the Bursary should remain an internal company-funded scheme or be replaced, supplemented, or succeeded by another approved structure.
