# The EDrill Family Bursary - Future Fundraising Controls Placeholder

Status: Future placeholder only  
Purpose: define the control questions and minimum safeguards required before The EDrill Family Bursary accepts employee/member contributions, donations, or any public fundraising support.

## 1. Current Position

Phase 1 of The EDrill Family Bursary is designed as a company-funded internal discretionary education support scheme.

Phase 1 must not:

- solicit donations;
- accept employee/member contributions;
- run public appeals;
- use crowdfunding;
- represent itself as a charity, foundation, registered society, or public fund;
- suggest that funds are being raised from the public or employees.

## 2. Why This Placeholder Exists

If the Bursary "catches on", EDrill may wish to raise money from employees, members, or a wider community. That future step could create regulatory, tax, accounting, governance, donor communication, and reputational obligations.

This document preserves the pathway without activating it.

## 3. Fundraising Trigger Events

Fundraising review is required before any of the following:

- asking employees to contribute money;
- asking members to pay into a bursary fund;
- inviting voluntary payroll deductions;
- publishing a donation appeal;
- accepting public donations;
- selling merchandise where proceeds support the Bursary;
- using crowdfunding platforms;
- collecting funds for Thailand, Myanmar, or other non-Singapore beneficiaries;
- holding events where proceeds go to the Bursary.

## 4. Key Legal Questions

Before any fundraising begins, EDrill should confirm:

1. Who is raising the funds?
2. Who legally receives and owns the funds?
3. Are the funds company funds, society funds, charity funds, or trust funds?
4. Are the contributions private/internal or public?
5. Are the purposes charitable, benevolent, philanthropic, mutual aid, or employee welfare?
6. Are the beneficiaries in Singapore or overseas?
7. Is a permit, disclosure, charity registration, ROS filing, or other approval required?
8. Are donor tax deductions being offered or implied?
9. What records and accounts must be kept?
10. What reporting is required after the appeal?

## 5. Minimum Controls Before Accepting Contributions

No contribution should be accepted until these are approved:

| Control | Required before fundraising |
| --- | --- |
| Legal structure | Confirm society, company, charity, trust, or internal fund |
| Fund owner | Confirm who legally receives and controls money |
| Bank account | Confirm dedicated account or accounting treatment |
| Purpose statement | Confirm permitted purpose and beneficiary scope |
| Fundraising approval | Confirm whether permit/disclosure/notification is required |
| Donor communication | Confirm wording and disclaimers |
| Finance controls | Confirm receipting, accounting, signatories, reconciliation |
| Expense controls | Confirm fundraising cost limits and approval |
| Distribution controls | Confirm how funds are applied to bursaries |
| Reporting | Confirm donor/member/regulator reporting |
| Data protection | Confirm donor and beneficiary data handling |

## 6. Public Versus Private Fundraising

The future model must distinguish:

- private internal contributions from a closed group;
- public fundraising appeals;
- fundraising for local charitable purposes;
- fundraising for foreign charitable purposes;
- member subscriptions;
- donations;
- sponsorships.

These categories may have different legal consequences.

## 7. Foreign Beneficiary Risk

If funds are raised in Singapore for children or families in Thailand, Myanmar, or other countries, EDrill must check whether foreign charitable purpose fundraising rules apply.

Before any such appeal, obtain advice on:

- permit requirements;
- 80:20 rule or waiver relevance;
- beneficiary documentation;
- accounts and audit requirements;
- post-appeal reporting;
- fund transmission records;
- donor disclosure requirements.

## 8. Donor / Contributor Communication Rules

Any future fundraising communication should clearly state:

- who is collecting funds;
- whether the collector is a registered society, charity, company, or other body;
- the purpose of funds;
- who the intended beneficiaries are;
- whether donations are tax-deductible;
- how funds will be used;
- whether administrative or fundraising costs may be deducted;
- how donors can receive updates;
- who to contact with questions.

Do not imply charity registration, IPC status, tax deductibility, or official approval unless confirmed.

## 9. Financial Controls

A future fundraising model should include:

- dedicated ledger or bank account;
- at least two authorised approvers;
- segregation of duties;
- receipt records;
- donor/contributor register;
- expense approval rules;
- reconciliation schedule;
- annual financial report;
- audit or independent review where required;
- restriction on personal benefit;
- remaining-funds rule on closure or dissolution.

## 10. Fundraising Expense Control

Fundraising costs must be controlled and transparent.

Before using third-party fundraisers, platforms, events, merchandise, or paid campaigns, confirm:

- whether the arrangement is allowed;
- whether a written agreement is required;
- what fees or costs will be deducted;
- what disclosures must be made;
- who is responsible for donor complaints;
- what records must be kept.

## 11. Use of Funds

Funds should only be used for approved bursary purposes.

Rules should specify:

- eligible beneficiaries;
- eligible education costs;
- selection process;
- payment route;
- unused funds;
- emergency or hardship exceptions;
- administrative costs;
- reporting back to donors or members.

## 12. Records

Future fundraising records should include:

- approval to conduct appeal;
- permit/disclosure records, if applicable;
- appeal period;
- appeal wording;
- donor/contributor records;
- gross funds raised;
- expenses;
- net funds available;
- bursary disbursements;
- overseas transfers, if any;
- receipts and acknowledgements;
- closing statement of account.

## 13. Decision Required Before Activation

The Executive Sponsor, Finance, Legal/Compliance, and any future society committee must approve the fundraising model before it is activated.

| Decision | Owner | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal structure selected | Executive Sponsor / Legal | Open |
| Fundraising type selected | Legal / Finance | Open |
| Beneficiary geography confirmed | Executive Sponsor / HR | Open |
| Permit/disclosure requirements confirmed | Legal / Compliance | Open |
| Bank/accounting model confirmed | Finance | Open |
| Donor communication approved | Legal / Communications | Open |
| Reporting model approved | Finance / Legal | Open |

## 14. Recommended Position

Do not accept contributions until the future legal structure and fundraising controls are approved.

If employees want to support the Bursary before that point, the safest interim response is:

> Thank you for the interest. The first phase of the Bursary is company-funded only. We are not accepting contributions at this stage. If the funding model changes in future, we will communicate the approved process.
