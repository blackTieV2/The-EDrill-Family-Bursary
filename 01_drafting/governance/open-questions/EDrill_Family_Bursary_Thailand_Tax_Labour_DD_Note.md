# The EDrill Family Bursary - Thailand Tax and Labour DD Note

Status: Working due diligence note  
Date: 11 June 2026  
Purpose: record public-source due diligence on Thailand tax/payroll and labour-hire issues before launch.

Important: This note is not legal or tax advice. It identifies risk and recommended questions for Thai Finance/Payroll/Legal or an external Thai adviser.

---

## 1. Executive Conclusion

The tax position has now been answered for drafting purposes: Bursary payments are tax liable to the recipient, and the recipient is responsible for their own tax position.

The safer working assumption is:

1. A bursary payment to an EDrill employee's bank account should be treated as recipient-taxable and should not be described as tax-free.
2. A payment to a labour-hire agency for onward payment to its employee may create separate tax, accounting, labour-hire, and evidence issues.
3. EDrill should process the Bursary only after Finance/Payroll confirms withholding, reporting, payslip, year-end certificate, and accounting treatment.
4. The documents should avoid saying "tax-free", "non-taxable", "scholarship", or "welfare benefit" as a legal conclusion.

The scheme can still proceed, but the launch gate is not closed until Finance/Payroll confirms how recipient-taxable payments will be processed, recorded, reported, and evidenced.

## 2. Public Source Findings

### 2.1 Thailand Revenue Code - broad employment income concept

The Thai Revenue Department's English Revenue Code materials state that assessable income includes property or any other benefit received that can be computed into money value.

Section 40(1) covers income from employment, including salary, wages, bonus, allowances, employer payment of an employee's debt liability, or any money, property, or benefit derived from employment.

Practical implication:

- A company-funded education support payment connected to the employee relationship could fall within employment income unless an exemption or different treatment applies.
- The fact that the purpose is education does not, by itself, prove non-taxable treatment.
- The risk is higher where the award is paid directly to the employee's bank account.
- Current drafting should proceed on the confirmed position that payments are tax liable to the recipient.

### 2.2 Thailand Revenue Code - education award exemption is not clearly a fit

Section 42 includes an exemption for an award for the purpose of education or technical research.

Practical implication:

- This may look relevant, but the public English text does not conclusively answer whether an employer's bursary for an employee's child qualifies, especially where the award is tied to employment and paid to the employee or through a labour-hire agency.
- EDrill should not rely on this exemption without Thai tax advice.

### 2.3 Child education allowance rules are separate

Thai Revenue Department public materials also refer to child and education allowances available to individual taxpayers in calculating personal income tax.

Practical implication:

- Those allowances are personal tax deductions/allowances for taxpayers. They do not automatically make employer-paid education support non-taxable.
- The Bursary documents should not confuse employee personal tax allowances with the company's payment treatment.

### 2.4 Withholding and reporting

The Revenue Department materials identify withholding tax provisions and employment income categories.

Practical implication:

- If the bursary is assessable employment income or a taxable benefit, EDrill may need to handle withholding/reporting through payroll or another approved route.
- If payment is made through a labour-hire agency, Finance must confirm whether EDrill, the agency, or both have reporting or documentation obligations.

### 2.5 Labour-hire / outsourced workers

Thailand's Labour Protection Act includes Section 11/1. The English translation reviewed states that where an entrepreneur entrusts another person to recruit persons to work as part of the entrepreneur's business operation, the entrepreneur may be deemed an employer of such workers for the relevant labour-protection purpose. It also states that contract employees performing work in the same manner as employees under employment contracts should enjoy fair benefits and welfare without discrimination.

Practical implication:

- The fact that labour-hire crew hold contracts with an agency does not fully remove EDrill labour-law risk.
- If EDrill offers a bursary to direct employees and selected labour-hire personnel, the criteria should be objective, consistently applied, and carefully worded.
- Payment via the agency is a sensible control, but it does not eliminate the need for Thai labour advice.

## 3. Recommended Drafting Position

Use this wording in operating documents:

> Any Bursary payment will be processed subject to applicable tax, payroll, withholding, reporting, accounting, and legal requirements. The Company may deduct, withhold, report, adjust, suspend, or recover amounts where required by law, internal controls, or the approved Bursary rules.

Avoid:

- "tax-free bursary"
- "non-taxable welfare"
- "school scholarship" as a legal label
- "gift"
- "entitlement"
- "guaranteed benefit"
- "labour-hire agency employees are our employees"

Use:

- "company-funded discretionary education support"
- "eligible employees"
- "eligible employees of the appointed labour-hire agency at the time of application who are assigned to EDrill rigs"
- "subject to applicable tax/payroll treatment"
- "tax liable to the recipient"
- "subject to evidence and annual approval"

## 4. Payment Route Risk Assessment

| Route | Practical benefit | Key risk | DD position |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EDrill pays employee bank account | Simple for employees and aligns with user instruction | Recipient-taxable payment; company must still confirm withholding/reporting mechanics | Allowed only after Payroll confirms processing; require school evidence and quarterly controls |
| EDrill pays school directly | Strong education-purpose evidence | More administrative; school may not accept third-party payments | Could be a future fallback or exception |
| EDrill reimburses employee against receipts | Stronger evidence than cash award | Still may be employment-related benefit | Could be fallback if direct bank route creates tax issue |
| EDrill pays labour-hire agency for onward payment | Preserves agency relationship better than direct payment to agency employee | Agency must actually pass payment through; labour/tax/accounting duties unclear | Require agency acknowledgement, onward-payment confirmation, and Thai advice |

## 5. Questions For Thai Finance / Payroll / Tax Adviser

The headline tax question has been answered: all Bursary payments are tax liable to the recipient, and the recipient is responsible for their own tax affairs.

Remaining implementation questions:

1. Should the recipient-taxable payment be processed through payroll or accounts payable?
2. Is company withholding required?
3. Should the payment appear on payslips or year-end tax certificates?
4. Does evidence of school enrolment, invoices, books, or uniforms change the company-side processing or reporting?
5. Would processing differ if the company reimbursed only against receipts?
6. Would processing differ if the company paid schools directly?
7. How should a payment to a labour-hire agency for onward payment to its employee be treated for tax, withholding, and accounting?
8. Does the labour-hire agency need to withhold or report anything when it pays its employee?
9. What written acknowledgement should the labour-hire agency provide?
10. Can the company deduct the cost for corporate tax purposes, and what evidence is required?
11. Are there any work-rule, welfare committee, labour-office, or employment-policy implications?
12. Does Section 11/1 of the Labour Protection Act create any fair-benefits/welfare issue for direct employees versus labour-hire crew?
13. Does the 12-month / continuous-service eligibility rule need adjustment for labour-hire crew?
14. What Thai-language wording should be used in employee-facing materials?

## 6. Recommended Launch Gate

Before launch, Finance/Payroll/Legal should issue a short written position covering:

1. payment route;
2. payment processor;
3. tax classification;
4. withholding/reporting treatment;
5. payslip/year-end reporting;
6. labour-hire agency onward-payment controls;
7. required evidence;
8. approved employee-facing wording.

Until that position is received, the Bursary should remain in draft/pre-launch status.
