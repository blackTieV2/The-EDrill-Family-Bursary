## 1. Executive Summary

The current document pack is substantially complete as a draft operating pack for an internal, company-funded, discretionary Thailand bursary.

The Bursary is not yet operational.

The principal reason is not lack of paperwork. Confirmed management decisions have closed or reclassified several earlier blockers. The remaining work is final launch administration and approval recording:

1. Record EDPL as the funding entity and keep cost centre / GL code as a Finance administrative processing detail.
2. Use the approved EDrill employee payroll route: normal payroll session, employee bank account, payslip line item, recipient-taxable treatment.
3. Use the Chairman/Committee-approved internal compliance position for consent, confidentiality, data minimisation, anonymised review where practicable, and restricted access controls.
4. Use the approved labour-hire agency model: EDPL pays the appointed agency for onward payment through the agency's existing payroll/payment process.
5. Complete conflict-of-interest and confidentiality declarations before Committee review/decision activity.
6. Complete final document checks and launch evidence/admin setup.
7. Approve internal and external communications before issue.

The long-term Singapore ROS / fundraising pathway has been preserved in scope, but deliberately not activated. That is the right posture for Phase 1. A Singapore society or employee-funded structure should only be pursued after separate legal, governance, fundraising, banking, tax, and membership decisions.

## 6. Critical Assumptions to Challenge

These assumptions should be tested before launch:

1. "A constitution is needed now."  
   For Phase 1, this may be the wrong frame. A company-funded internal bursary normally needs a charter, approval note, SOP, payment controls, privacy wording, and records. A society constitution becomes relevant only if a separate member body is formed later.

2. "The bursary is just a benefit and can be paid informally."
   That would be too loose. EDrill employee awards use the normal payroll session and appear as payslip line items. Payments are tax liable to the recipient.

3. "Including labour-hire agency employees is straightforward."  
   It may be acceptable, but the wording and process matter. The scheme should not imply labour-hire agency staff are EDrill employees or create unintended employment, agency, benefit, or co-employment signals.

4. "A LinkedIn post is harmless because it is good news."  
   Public wording can create legal and reputational risk if it implies a public charity, fundraising initiative, guaranteed entitlement, or identifiable child beneficiary without consent.

5. "Future employee fundraising can be added later with minor edits."  
   That is likely wrong. Employee/member fundraising may require a materially different structure, governance model, accounts, approvals, fundraising controls, and possibly registration or permits.

6. Revise all draft documents to approved issue versions when instructed.
7. Complete final Chairman/Committee/Communications pre-publication check.
8. Issue the internal launch message and FAQ.
9. Open applications only after the checklist supports launch.
10. Run committee review, document conflicts, approve awards, and issue award letters.
11. Publish the LinkedIn post only after launch/publication wording is cleared.
12. Complete an annual scheme report after the first award cycle.

---

## 8. Do Not Do Before Approval

Before the launch checklist is closed, do not:

1. Describe the Phase 1 Bursary as a charity, foundation, society, public fund, or ROS-registered body.
2. Ask employees, contractors, vendors, or the public for donations.
3. Open applications.
4. Promise an award to any employee, child, or labour-hire agency participant.
5. Make bursary payments.
6. Publish the LinkedIn post.
7. Use child names, photos, school names, or stories publicly without specific written consent.
8. State or imply the bursary is tax-free.
9. Refer to labour-hire agency staff as EDrill employees.
10. Treat renewal as automatic.
